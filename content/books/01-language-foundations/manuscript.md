## Publication and learning note

This is an original educational workbook for independent adult and young-adult learners. It is designed to introduce practical beginner communication in English, French and Spanish. The level labels refer to learning targets inspired by the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR); this book is not an official CEFR examination or certification.

The three languages are treated as equal learning goals. You may work through one language first, or compare all three in parallel. Do not assume that a phrase in one language can always be translated word for word into another.

# Welcome / Bienvenue / Bienvenido

### English
You do not need to know a language before you begin learning it. You need a clear goal, useful practice and enough repetition to make new patterns familiar. This book helps you take your first steps in English, French and Spanish.

### Français
Vous n’avez pas besoin de connaître une langue pour commencer à l’apprendre. Il vous faut un objectif clair, une pratique utile et des répétitions régulières pour reconnaître les nouvelles structures. Ce livre vous accompagne dans vos premiers pas en anglais, en français et en espagnol.

### Español
No necesitas conocer un idioma para empezar a aprenderlo. Necesitas un objetivo claro, práctica útil y repetición regular para familiarizarte con las nuevas estructuras. Este libro te acompaña en tus primeros pasos en inglés, francés y español.

## How to use this book / Comment utiliser ce livre / Cómo utilizar este libro

**English**
- Choose one language as your main focus for each study session.
- Read the matching explanation and say every example aloud.
- Compare the other two languages to notice similarities and differences.
- Complete the exercises before checking the answer key.
- Keep a record of useful words and sentences.
- Return to earlier units for short review sessions.

**Français**
- Choisissez une langue principale pour chaque séance.
- Lisez l’explication correspondante et prononcez chaque exemple à voix haute.
- Comparez les deux autres langues pour repérer les ressemblances et les différences.
- Faites les exercices avant de consulter les réponses.
- Notez les mots et les phrases utiles.
- Revenez régulièrement aux unités précédentes pour réviser.

**Español**
- Elige un idioma principal para cada sesión.
- Lee la explicación correspondiente y pronuncia cada ejemplo en voz alta.
- Compara los otros dos idiomas para observar semejanzas y diferencias.
- Haz los ejercicios antes de consultar las respuestas.
- Anota las palabras y frases útiles.
- Vuelve a las unidades anteriores para repasarlas.

## Suggested four-week plan

| Week | Focus | Suggested work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Units 1–3 | Learning routine, alphabet, spelling and first sounds |
| 2 | Units 4–5 | Greetings, politeness and personal introductions |

Study for 20–30 minutes on most days. Short, regular practice is usually more sustainable than one long weekly session.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this book, you should be able to:
- recognize and name letters in the three alphabets;
- spell a name and a simple email address;
- recognize several important pronunciation patterns;
- greet someone and use basic polite expressions;
- give a short personal introduction;
- exchange simple information about numbers, dates and time;
- ask and answer basic personal questions;
- understand common classroom instructions;
- build simple statements, questions and negative sentences.

# Unit 1 — Start Here: How Languages Work
## Unité 1 — Bien commencer : comment fonctionne l’apprentissage des langues
## Unidad 1 — Primeros pasos: cómo funciona el aprendizaje de idiomas

**Estimated time:** 45–60 minutes  
**Level:** Pre-A1  
**Main outcome:** create a realistic learning routine and understand how to use this workbook.

### 1.1 Can-do outcomes / Objectifs / Objetivos

**English:** I can choose a practical learning goal, organize a short study session and identify the four basic language skills.

**Français :** Je peux choisir un objectif pratique, organiser une courte séance et reconnaître les quatre compétences linguistiques fondamentales.

**Español:** Puedo elegir un objetivo práctico, organizar una sesión breve e identificar las cuatro destrezas lingüísticas fundamentales.

### 1.2 The four skills / Les quatre compétences / Las cuatro destrezas

| Skill | English | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | Understand spoken language | Compréhension orale | Comprensión auditiva |
| Speaking | Produce spoken language | Expression orale | Expresión oral |
| Reading | Understand written language | Compréhension écrite | Comprensión lectora |
| Writing | Produce written language | Expression écrite | Expresión escrita |

**English explanation:** Language learning becomes more useful when the four skills support one another. For example, listen to a short greeting, read it, repeat it and then write a similar greeting.

**Explication française :** L’apprentissage devient plus concret lorsque les quatre compétences se complètent. Par exemple, écoutez une salutation, lisez-la, répétez-la, puis écrivez une salutation semblable.

**Explicación en español:** El aprendizaje resulta más práctico cuando las cuatro destrezas se complementan. Por ejemplo, escucha un saludo breve, léelo, repítelo y después escribe un saludo parecido.

### 1.3 Set a useful goal / Définir un objectif utile / Establecer un objetivo útil

A useful beginner goal describes an action, not a vague ambition.

| Less useful | More useful |
|---|---|
| I want to know English. | I want to introduce myself in English in five sentences. |
| Je veux apprendre le français. | Je veux saluer une personne et lui demander son nom en français. |
| Quiero saber español. | Quiero decir de dónde soy y qué idiomas hablo en español. |

**Try it:** Complete one sentence for each language.

- English: In four weeks, I want to be able to ____________________.
- Français : Dans quatre semaines, je veux pouvoir ____________________.
- Español: En cuatro semanas, quiero poder ____________________.

### 1.4 A simple study cycle / Un cycle simple / Un ciclo sencillo

Use this 20-minute routine:

1. **Review — 3 minutes:** recall five words or phrases from the previous session.
2. **Notice — 5 minutes:** study one small pattern or sound.
3. **Practice — 7 minutes:** complete an exercise and say examples aloud.
4. **Use — 3 minutes:** write or say something personal.
5. **Record — 2 minutes:** note what was easy and what needs review.

### 1.5 Learning strategies

**English**
- Learn phrases in context rather than memorizing disconnected word lists.
- Repeat useful material on different days.
- Make mistakes, notice them and try again.
- Use recordings to compare your speech over time.

**Français**
- Apprenez les expressions dans leur contexte plutôt que sous forme de listes isolées.
- Révisez le contenu à plusieurs jours d’intervalle.
- Faites des erreurs, observez-les et recommencez.
- Utilisez des enregistrements pour suivre votre prononciation.

**Español**
- Aprende expresiones dentro de un contexto, no solo listas de palabras aisladas.
- Repasa el contenido en distintos días.
- Comete errores, obsérvalos e inténtalo de nuevo.
- Utiliza grabaciones para comparar tu pronunciación con el tiempo.

### 1.6 Practice

**Exercise A — Match the skill.** Write L for listening, S for speaking, R for reading and W for writing.

1. You understand a recorded greeting. ___
2. You write your name on a form. ___
3. You read a short sign. ___
4. You introduce yourself aloud. ___

**Exercise B — Choose a goal.** Which goal is the most measurable?

a. I want to become fluent very quickly.  
b. I want to learn everything about French.  
c. I want to ask and answer three basic questions in French.

**Exercise C — Personal learning plan.** Write:
- your main language for this week;
- the number of days you will study;
- the time you can realistically study each day;
- one action you want to perform by the end of the week.

### 1.7 Communicative task

Explain your learning goal aloud in the language you are studying. If you are not ready to speak, write it first and read it slowly.

### 1.8 Self-check

- [ ] I can name the four language skills.
- [ ] I have one specific beginner goal.
- [ ] I have chosen a realistic study schedule.
- [ ] I know how to use the three-language examples.

# Unit 2 — The Alphabet and Spelling
## Unité 2 — L’alphabet et l’épellation
## Unidad 2 — El alfabeto y cómo deletrear

**Estimated time:** 60–75 minutes  
**Level:** Pre-A1  
**Main outcome:** recognize the letters, spell a name and ask someone to repeat or spell information.

### 2.1 Can-do outcomes

**English:** I can recognize the letters used in English, French and Spanish, spell my name and ask how a word is spelled.

**Français :** Je peux reconnaître les lettres utilisées en anglais, en français et en espagnol, épeler mon nom et demander comment un mot s’écrit.

**Español:** Puedo reconocer las letras del inglés, del francés y del español, deletrear mi nombre y preguntar cómo se escribe una palabra.

### 2.2 The three alphabets

English and French use 26 basic letters. Modern Spanish uses 27 letters because **ñ** is a separate letter. The letter combinations **ch** and **ll** are not separate letters in the modern Spanish alphabet.

| Letter | English name | French name | Spanish name |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | ay | a | a |
| B | bee | bé | be |
| C | see | cé | ce |
| D | dee | dé | de |
| E | ee | e | e |
| F | ef | effe | efe |
| G | gee | gé | ge |
| H | aitch | ache | hache |
| I | eye | i | i |
| J | jay | ji | jota |
| K | kay | ka | ka |
| L | el | elle | ele |
| M | em | emme | eme |
| N | en | enne | ene |
| Ñ | — | — | eñe |
| O | oh | o | o |
| P | pee | pé | pe |
| Q | cue | qu | cu |
| R | ar | erre | erre |
| S | ess | esse | ese |
| T | tee | té | te |
| U | you | u | u |
| V | vee | vé | uve |
| W | double-u | double vé | uve doble |
| X | ex | ix | equis |
| Y | why | i grec | ye / i griega |
| Z | zee / zed | zède | zeta |

**Pronunciation note:** English letter names vary by region. For example, **Z** is commonly called “zee” in US English and “zed” in UK English. Spanish names for **Y** also vary: “ye” and “i griega” are both used. Learners should recognize common variants.

### 2.3 Accents and written marks

These marks are important for reading and spelling. They do not all serve the same function.

| Mark | English | French | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acute accent | Used in some borrowed words and names | é | á, é, í, ó, ú |
| Grave accent | Not part of ordinary native spelling | à, è, ù | Not used as a Spanish accent mark |
| Circumflex | Used in some borrowed words | â, ê, î, ô, û | Not used |
| Cedilla | Not used in ordinary English | ç | Not used |
| Tilde | In some borrowed words | Not used as a French letter mark | ñ; also marks stress on vowels in certain words |
| Diaeresis | In some names/borrowings | ë, ï, ü, ÿ | ü in güe and güi sequences |

Examples:
- French: **école**, **garçon**, ** Noël**.
- Spanish: **año**, **niño**, **pingüino**, **teléfono**.
- English: names and borrowed words may preserve marks, but ordinary spelling does not use these systems in the same way.

### 2.4 Useful spelling questions

| English | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|
| How do you spell your name? | Comment épelez-vous votre nom ? | ¿Cómo se escribe tu nombre? |
| Could you spell that, please? | Pourriez-vous l’épeler, s’il vous plaît ? | ¿Podría deletrearlo, por favor? |
| Could you repeat that? | Pourriez-vous répéter ? | ¿Podría repetirlo? |
| Is that B or V? | Est-ce un B ou un V ? | ¿Es una B o una V? |
| How do you write that? | Comment cela s’écrit-il ? | ¿Cómo se escribe eso? |

Register note: the French **vous** and Spanish **usted** forms are polite/formal. Informal alternatives include **tu** and **tú**.

### 2.5 Spelling names and email addresses

When spelling an email address:
- say the letters slowly;
- identify numbers clearly;
- say the symbols using the conventions of the language;
- repeat the complete address to confirm it.

| Symbol | English | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| @ | at | arobase | arroba |
| . | dot / period | point | punto |
| _ | underscore | tiret bas | guion bajo |
| - | hyphen | tiret | guion |

The spoken conventions for email addresses can vary. If the listener looks uncertain, describe the symbol or repeat the address.

**Exercise C — Spell aloud.** Spell:

**Exercise D — Complete the question.**

1. English: How do you ______ your name?

### 2.7 Communicative task

Work alone or with a partner. Person A says a fictional name. Person B asks for the spelling, writes the letters and reads the name back. Change roles. Repeat in English, French and Spanish.

- [ ] I can recognize French accented letters.
- [ ] I know that ñ is a separate letter in Spanish.
- [ ] I can ask someone to spell a name.
- [ ] I can spell my name slowly and clearly.

# Unit 3 — First Sounds and Pronunciation
## Unité 3 — Premiers sons et prononciation
## Unidad 3 — Primeros sonidos y pronunciación

**Estimated time:** 60–75 minutes  
**Level:** Pre-A1–A1  
**Main outcome:** recognize basic vowel and stress patterns and practise clear, intelligible pronunciation.

### 3.1 Can-do outcomes

**English:** I can identify vowel letters, notice that spelling and sound do not always match, and practise word stress.

**Français :** Je peux reconnaître les voyelles, comprendre que l’orthographe ne correspond pas toujours directement aux sons et m’entraîner à l’accentuation.

**Español:** Puedo reconocer las vocales, observar que la escritura no siempre representa los sonidos de forma directa y practicar la acentuación.

### 3.2 Vowels: a useful first comparison

**English:** English has five main vowel letters (a, e, i, o, u), but many more vowel sounds. The pronunciation of a vowel can change with the word and accent. Learn the sound of a word together with its spelling.

**Français :** Le français utilise les lettres a, e, i, o et u pour écrire les voyelles orales, mais aussi des combinaisons de lettres et des voyelles nasales. La lettre **u** de *tu* n’a pas le même son que le **ou** de *vous*.

**Español:** El español tiene cinco sonidos vocálicos principales: **a, e, i, o, u**. En général, estos sonidos son más estables que las vocales del inglés. La pronunciación puede variar ligeramente según la región, pero las cinco vocales mantienen patrones bastante regulares.

### 3.3 Listen for the difference

| Language | Example | Learning focus |
|---|---|---|
| English | name / map | The letter a has different sounds. |
| French | tu / tout | Distinguish **u** and **ou**. |
| Spanish | casa / mesa / vino / solo / luna | Notice the five stable vowel patterns. |

Do not try to reproduce a sound from spelling alone. Listen to a reliable recording, repeat it, and compare your own recording.

### 3.4 Consonants and spelling patterns

Some letters have different sound values in different languages.

- **H:** usually silent in Spanish words such as *hola*; often pronounced in English words such as *hello*; generally silent in French words such as *homme*.
- **J:** has different pronunciations in English *job*, French *jour* and Spanish *jardín*.
- **R:** the sound differs considerably among English, French and Spanish accents.
- **C:** its pronunciation depends on the following letter and the language. Compare English *city*, French *cinéma* and Spanish *cena*.

These are introductory observations, not a complete phonetic description. Regional accents are normal; the goal is intelligibility.

### 3.5 Syllables and word stress

A syllable is a spoken beat in a word. Word stress makes one syllable more prominent.

Examples:
- English: **TA**ble, ho**TEL**.
- French: stress is commonly associated with the final pronounced syllable of a rhythmic group rather than fixed lexical stress in the same way as English or Spanish.
- Spanish: **CA**sa, ho**TEL**, te**LÉ**fono.

In Spanish, written accent marks help identify stress in words that do not follow the usual spelling rules. In French, accents also distinguish spelling and pronunciation patterns. In English, stress is often not marked in ordinary spelling, so learners need to hear it.

### 3.6 A five-step pronunciation routine

1. Listen to a complete word or short phrase.
2. Listen again and notice the vowel or stressed syllable.
3. Repeat slowly without exaggerating every sound.
4. Record yourself.
5. Compare your recording with the model and repeat one more time.
