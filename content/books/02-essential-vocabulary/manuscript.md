# Essential Vocabulary
## Build Your Everyday English, French and Spanish

**EduBooks · Multilingual Learning Series 01**  
**Target:** A1–A2 approximate learning target; not an official CEFR certification.  
**Learning and explanation languages:** English · Français · Español  
**Editorial status:** First complete working manuscript — review candidate, not approved for sale.

## Publication and learning note

This book develops practical, high-frequency vocabulary for familiar situations. It is designed to accompany conversation, listening, reading and writing practice. Vocabulary choices are a practical teaching selection, not a statistically ranked frequency list. English examples use widely understood international English; French uses standard contemporary French; Spanish uses broadly understood standard Spanish. Regional alternatives are identified when useful.

This is an independent learning resource, not an official CEFR publication, examination or certification.

## Welcome | Bienvenue | Bienvenido

**English.** Welcome to Essential Vocabulary. Learn words in useful expressions and real situations, not as isolated translations. Say examples aloud, personalise them and revisit earlier units regularly.

**Français.** Bienvenue dans Essential Vocabulary. Apprenez les mots dans des expressions utiles et des situations concrètes, et non comme de simples traductions isolées. Prononcez les exemples, adaptez-les à votre vie et révisez régulièrement les unités précédentes.

**Español.** Te damos la bienvenida a Essential Vocabulary. Aprende palabras dentro de expresiones útiles y situaciones reales, no como traducciones aisladas. Di los ejemplos en voz alta, adáptalos a tu vida y vuelve a las unidades anteriores con frecuencia.

## How to use this book | Comment utiliser ce livre | Cómo usar este libro

1. Read the situation before studying the vocabulary.
2. Learn a word with its article, common partner or typical phrase.
3. Cover the translation and retrieve the meaning from memory.
4. Make one true sentence and one question of your own.
5. Review after one day, three days, one week and two weeks.
6. Complete the unit task without looking at the examples, then check your work.

**Français.** Lisez d'abord la situation. Apprenez chaque mot avec son article ou une expression typique. Cachez la traduction, retrouvez le sens de mémoire, puis créez une phrase et une question personnelles. Révisez après un jour, trois jours, une semaine et deux semaines.

**Español.** Lee primero la situación. Aprende cada palabra con su artículo o una expresión típica. Tapa la traducción, recuerda el significado y crea una frase y una pregunta propias. Repasa al cabo de un día, tres días, una semana y dos semanas.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, learners should be able to:
- use core vocabulary for people, home, food, shopping and routines;
- ask for and give simple information about places, travel, work and study;
- describe basic health needs, weather, leisure activities and feelings;
- use common collocations and word families rather than translating word by word;
- notice selected cognates and false friends across the three languages;
- maintain a personal vocabulary record and use spaced retrieval.

## A note on the three languages

The tables present useful equivalents, not a guarantee that every word has the same range of meaning. Grammatical gender, articles, plural forms, prepositions and idiomatic combinations differ. Learn each expression as a complete unit. Where a noun is shown with an article, keep the article attached while learning.

# Unit 1 — People, Family and Relationships
## Unité 1 — Les personnes, la famille et les relations
## Unidad 1 — Las personas, la familia y las relaciones

**Can-do:** introduce people, describe a relationship and ask simple questions about someone's family.

### Discover

**English:** This is my sister, Lina. She lives with her partner and their two children. We visit our grandparents on Sundays.

**Français:** Voici ma sœur, Lina. Elle habite avec son compagnon et leurs deux enfants. Nous rendons visite à nos grands-parents le dimanche.

**Español:** Esta es mi hermana, Lina. Vive con su pareja y sus dos hijos. Visitamos a nuestros abuelos los domingos.

| a family | une famille | una familia |

| a father | un père | un padre |
| a sister | une sœur | una hermana |
| a brother | un frère | un hermano |
| a child / children | un enfant / des enfants | un niño / una niña; niños |
| a couple | un couple | una pareja |
| a partner | un partenaire / une partenaire | una pareja |
| grandparents | les grands-parents | los abuelos |
| a neighbour | un voisin / une voisine | un vecino / una vecina |
| a friend | un ami / une amie | un amigo / una amiga |
| a colleague | un collègue / une collègue | un compañero / una compañera de trabajo |

### Descriptions and useful phrases

| English | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|
| friendly | sympathique | amable / simpático/a |
| kind | gentil / gentille | amable |
| funny | drôle | divertido/a |
| quiet | calme | tranquilo/a |
| helpful | serviable | servicial |
| to live with | vivre avec | vivir con |
| to get on well with | bien s'entendre avec | llevarse bien con |
| a close friend | un ami proche / une amie proche | un amigo cercano |
| a family member | un membre de la famille | un familiar |
| spend time together | passer du temps ensemble | pasar tiempo juntos |

### Understand and compare

- English: *my parents* uses a possessive determiner before a plural noun.
- French: *mes parents* uses *mes* with a plural noun; the possessive agrees with the noun, not the owner.
- Spanish: *mis padres* uses *mis* before a plural noun; *mi* becomes *mis* in the plural.

Examples: *My brother is kind. / Mon frère est gentil. / Mi hermano es amable.*  
*We get on well. / Nous nous entendons bien. / Nos llevamos bien.*

### Pronunciation focus

The English **th** in *mother* is not the same sound as **t** or **d**. In French, final consonants are often silent, but not always. In Spanish, written vowels are generally pronounced clearly and consistently. Use a reliable audio model when available; do not infer pronunciation from spelling alone.

### Guided practice

1. My mother's brother is my ______.
2. She lives (with / to) her parents.
3. Complete in French: “my sisters” = ______ sœurs.
4. Complete in Spanish: “my brothers” = ______ hermanos.
5. Match *a colleague / un collègue / un compañero de trabajo*.
6. Write two sentences describing a real or imaginary family.
7. Ask a partner: “Who do you live with?” / “Avec qui habites-tu ?” / “¿Con quién vives?”

### Communicate

Introduce two people. Say who they are, how they are related and one quality for each person. Do not share private information you do not wish to disclose.

### Self-check
- [ ] I can name at least eight family or relationship words.
- [ ] I can describe two people.
- [ ] I can ask who someone lives with.

### Understand and compare

- English: “There is a lamp on the table.” Use *there is* for one item and *there are* for plural items.
- French: “Il y a une lampe sur la table.” *Il y a* works with singular and plural.
- Spanish: “Hay una lámpara en la mesa.” *Hay* expresses existence; *estar* commonly locates a specific object.

Compare: *There is a book on the desk. / Il y a un livre sur le bureau. / Hay un libro en el escritorio.*  
*The book is on the desk. / Le livre est sur le bureau. / El libro está en el escritorio.*

5. Name five objects in a room near you.
6. Write three sentences describing an imaginary bedroom.
7. Ask “Where is the bathroom?” in all three languages.

### Communicate

Describe a room without naming it. Your partner guesses the room from the objects and their positions. Use at least five nouns and three location phrases.

### Quantities and useful language

| English | Français | Español |

| a glass of water | un verre d'eau | un vaso de agua |

- I'd like …, please. / Je voudrais …, s'il vous plaît. / Quisiera …, por favor.
- Could I have …? / Est-ce que je pourrais avoir … ? / ¿Me podría traer …?
- What do you recommend? / Que recommandez-vous ? / ¿Qué recomienda?
- I'm allergic to … / Je suis allergique à … / Soy alérgico/a a …

### Understand and compare

English distinguishes countable nouns (*an apple, two apples*) and uncountable nouns (*bread, rice*). French uses partitive articles such as *du, de la, de l'* in many positive food statements. Spanish often uses articles or no article depending on whether the noun is specific, general or part of a meal expression.

Examples: *I eat rice. / Je mange du riz. / Como arroz.*  
*I would like an apple. / Je voudrais une pomme. / Quisiera una manzana.*

5. Write three foods you eat for breakfast.

### Communicate

# Unit 12 — Build Your Vocabulary System
## Unité 12 — Construire son système de vocabulaire
## Unidad 12 — Crear tu sistema de vocabulario

**Can-do:** learn vocabulary in meaningful groups, recognise selected word relationships and plan independent review.

### 1. Learn collocations

A collocation is a combination of words that commonly occur together. Learning the combination makes language more natural.

| English | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|
| make a decision | prendre une décision | tomar una decisión |
| do homework | faire ses devoirs | hacer los deberes |
| take a photo | prendre une photo | hacer una foto |
| heavy rain | forte pluie | lluvia intensa |
| strong coffee | café fort | café fuerte |
| pay attention | faire attention | prestar atención |
| keep a promise | tenir une promesse | cumplir una promesa |
| catch a bus | prendre le bus | coger / tomar el autobús |
| save time | gagner du temps | ahorrar tiempo |
| ask a question | poser une question | hacer una pregunta |

Notice that the verb changes across languages. English *make a decision* is French *prendre une décision* and Spanish *tomar una decisión*. Avoid translating the verb mechanically.

### 2. Notice word families

| English family | Français | Español |
|---|---|---|
| act · action · active | agir · action · actif | actuar · acción · activo |
| decide · decision | décider · décision | decidir · decisión |
| educate · education | éduquer · éducation | educar · educación |
| inform · information | informer · information | informar · información |
| organise · organisation | organiser · organisation | organizar · organización |
| create · creation · creative | créer · création · créatif | crear · creación · creativo |

Word families help recognition, but spelling and pronunciation can change. Check the grammatical category: a noun, verb and adjective do different jobs in a sentence.

### 3. Cognates and false friends

Cognates are words related in form and meaning across languages. Similarity is not proof of identical use.

| English | Français | Español | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| information | information | información | Similar meaning; spelling differs. |
| important | important | importante | Cognate; pronunciation differs. |
| possible | possible | posible | Cognate; spelling differs. |
| family | famille | familia | Related meaning; forms differ. |
| restaurant | restaurant | restaurante | Similar form and meaning. |

False friends look or sound similar but differ in meaning or use.

| Similar-looking words | Actual meaning |
|---|---|
| English *actually* / French *actuellement* | English: in fact; French: currently. |
| English *library* / French *librairie* | English: place to borrow books; French *librairie*: bookshop. |
| English *assist* / Spanish *asistir* | English: help; Spanish *asistir a*: attend an event or class. |
| English *embarrassed* / Spanish *embarazada* | English: feeling awkward; Spanish: pregnant. |
| English *sensible* / French *sensible* | English: reasonable; French: sensitive. |
| English *large* / French *large* | English: big; French commonly: wide. |

Confirm context and a current dictionary entry before relying on a similar-looking word.

### 4. Use a vocabulary notebook

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Headword | decision |
| Meaning | a choice made after thinking |
| Collocation | make a decision |
| Example | I need to make a decision today. |
| Related word | decide (verb) |
| Personal link | a decision about my study plan |
| Review dates | tomorrow · day 3 · day 7 · day 14 |

### 5. Spaced retrieval

- **Day 0:** learn the word in a phrase and create an example.
- **Day 1:** recall it without looking.
- **Day 3:** use it in a new sentence.
- **Day 7:** answer a question or speak for one minute using it.
- **Day 14:** test yourself again and move difficult items back to a shorter interval.

If you cannot recall an item, look at the answer, use it once and schedule another review. Forgetting is a signal to practise, not a reason to copy the word repeatedly.

### Guided practice

1. Match *make a decision* with its natural French and Spanish equivalents.
2. Choose the correct collocation: (do / make) homework.
3. Is *library* the same as French *librairie*? Explain.
4. Write two words from the family *decide / decision* and identify their grammatical roles.
5. Add five new words to the vocabulary notebook format.
6. Write a personal sentence with *pay attention*.
7. Create a seven-day review plan for ten new words.

### Communicate

Teach a partner five words from this book. For each, give its meaning, a natural word partner, an example sentence and one memory clue. Then ask the partner to recall the words without looking.

### Self-check
- [ ] I can recognise common collocations.
- [ ] I can identify some word families.
- [ ] I can explain why a false friend can cause confusion.
- [ ] I can maintain a vocabulary notebook and review schedule.

# Glossary of Learning Terms

- **Article:** a word used with a noun, such as *a, the, le, la, un, una*.
- **Collocation:** words that commonly occur together.
- **Cognate:** a word related in form and meaning to a word in another language.
- **False friend:** a similar-looking word with a different meaning or use.
- **Frequency adverb:** a word such as *usually* or *often* that describes how frequently something happens.
- **Word family:** words related by form and meaning, often belonging to different grammatical categories.
- **Retrieval practice:** trying to recall information from memory rather than only rereading it.
- **Spaced review:** revisiting information at increasing intervals.
