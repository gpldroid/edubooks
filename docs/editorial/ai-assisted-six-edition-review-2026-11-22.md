## 2. AI-assisted language review

### Book 01 — Language Foundations

- EN: The manuscript follows a coherent beginner sequence from learning strategies and alphabet/spelling through pronunciation, greetings, personal information, basic sentences and an integrated task. Examples and short answer forms are broadly consistent with the stated Pre-A1–A1 target. A language editor should specifically verify consistent use of British English conventions (the PDF metadata currently uses `en-GB`), natural classroom instructions, and whether pronunciation examples are sufficiently precise without audio.
- FR: The manuscript uses a formal learner-facing register in several instructional sections. A French editor should check consistency between `vous` forms, imperative forms and the tone of learner prompts; verify naturalness of phonetic/spelling terminology and avoid overly literal equivalents of English language-learning terminology.
- ES: The manuscript uses a largely neutral instructional register. A Spanish editor should check consistency of `tú` address, accentuation, punctuation, and regional neutrality (for example, vocabulary choices that may differ between Spain and Latin America).
- The existing Book 01 answer-key reconciliation reports one corrected French Unit 3 mismatch. That correction should remain in the final generated PDF.

### Book 02 — Essential Vocabulary

- EN: The twelve-topic everyday vocabulary sequence is useful for self-study. A language editor should review collocations and countability, article use, phrasal/multiword expressions, and the stated British-English convention. Check that regional alternatives are marked where relevant.
- FR: The manuscript should be reviewed for consistency of `tu`/ `vous`, gender and article presentation, register, collocations and regional vocabulary. The existing answer-key cross-check corrected the weather prompt in Unit 10 so that both accepted completions are idiomatic.
- ES: Review `ser`/ `estar`, agreement, articles, collocations and the consistency of the neutral `tú` register. The existing answer-key cross-check narrowed the Unit 9 body-part prompt so the supplied answer is unambiguous.
- The health-related unit is framed as language practice rather than medical advice; retain that limitation in the final edition.

### Cross-language result

No obvious placeholder markers or source URLs were found by the automated scan. This is not evidence that the manuscripts are free of unattributed quotations, paraphrases, copied examples, trademark issues or factual errors. AI review cannot certify native-level idiomaticity.

## 3. AI-assisted pedagogical review

### Strengths observed

- Book 01 moves from foundational language awareness to controlled practice and an integrated beginner task.
- Book 02 organizes vocabulary by everyday communicative domains and includes vocabulary in context, guided practice, answer checks, communication tasks, self-checks and spaced-review guidance.
- Both books distinguish open responses from closed-answer exercises and avoid requiring learners to disclose private personal information.
- Level statements are qualified as approximate learning targets, not official CEFR certification.
- Existing answer-key reports document a first-pass comparison of ten units × three languages in Book 01 and twelve units × three languages in Book 02.

### Required editorial improvements before final approval

1. Make learning outcomes measurable and map each unit's practice task to one or more stated outcomes.
2. Add a consistent answer-key convention for acceptable alternatives, spelling variants and partially correct responses.
3. Ensure every open task has a concise scoring rubric or model answer; avoid generic “open task” notes where a more specific success criterion is needed.
4. Verify the vocabulary selection against the stated A1/A2 learning range; distinguish receptive recognition from productive use.
5. Add explicit pronunciation/audio limitations and alternative practice guidance where a sound cannot be demonstrated in print.
6. Check that examples do not accidentally present culturally narrow assumptions as universal.
7. Keep the health unit's non-medical-advice disclaimer.
8. Ensure all language editions have parallel instructional depth without forcing literal translation where a natural target-language formulation is better.
